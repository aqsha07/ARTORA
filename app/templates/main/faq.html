What is the difference between Belgian Linen and Cotton Canvas?

Linen is woven from long-strand European flax fibers, which are naturally more resilient, flexible, and resistant to atmospheric moisture shifts than cotton. Over decades or centuries, linen remains taut and retains structural integrity. Cotton is softer with a more uniform weave, making it ideal for daily studies and contemporary impasto work.

Why do your watercolors use honey as a binder?

Honey acts as a natural humectant that prevents pans from turning rock-hard or brittle in dry climates. A honey-bound watercolor reactivates instantly upon the lightest touch of a moist brush and produces rich, creamy washes with superior luminosity.

How should I care for my Kolinsky Sable and Hog Bristle brushes?

Never rest brushes head-down in water or solvent jars, as this permanently bends the bristles and loosens ferrule adhesives. Wash gently with lukewarm water and pure vegetable soap, reshape the point with your fingers, and dry flat on a horizontal towel.

What is your packaging standard for fragile ceramic dishes and heavy canvases?

All canvases are encased in heavy corrugated corner guards and moisture-barrier film. Ceramic palettes and glass mediums are cocooned in honeycomb biodegradable cushioning and double-walled cartons.
